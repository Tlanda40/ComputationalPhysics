    \subsubsection{Problem 2.}\label{problem-2.}

\emph{(a)} See figure 5. The scale height of the atmosphere (10 km)
might be relevant because it allows us to infer whether or not a muon
came from space based on its speed / energy!?

\emph{(b)} I addressed parametrization of speeds as follows: for the
linear scale plots I instantiated a linearly spaced array of gamma
values, and then computed the corresponding beta values. For the log
scale plots, I instantiated a logarithmically spaced array of gamma
values, which was great because I got a relatively high density of
points for the curved part of the figure without having an extremely
high resolution for the linear part. I also ran into issues with the
graph vs \(1-\beta\), because of catastrophic subtraction at high
relativistic speeds. This was resolved by computing \(1-\beta\) as
\[1-\beta=\frac{1-\beta^2}{1+\beta}=\frac{1}{\gamma^2\,(1+\beta)}.\]
Here, even if the computer (floating point) can't tell the difference
between \(\beta\) and \(1\), we still get an appropriately low number by
using division instead of subtraction.

    \subsubsection{Problem 4.}\label{problem-4.}

We perform four tests (figures 10-13): of the pythagorean identity, of
the odd parity of \(\sin\), of the double angle identity for \(\sin\),
and of the complementarity of \(\cos\) to \(\sin\) (that \(\cos\) is the
\(\sin\) of the complement). For the pythagorean identity, we can see
that errors appear at all scales, at roughly the same magnitude
(\(10^{-16}\), where float stops being precise as we know from earlier).
We see no errors in the parity of \(\sin\), which makes sense if that's
built into the numpy \(\sin\) function. For the latter two tests, I've
also plotted log-log plots, because I think they're informative. For the
double angle formula and the complementarity test, we see that the error
increases with scale, and the log-log tells us how more precisely; in
both graphs there's an interesting step-function-like behavior that
seems to indicate some kind of discretized power law for the error. In
the double angle test, the error tops out at order \(10^{-16}\), but the
error in the complementarity test seems to just increase, which is
probably because there's addition/subtraction involved between a big big
number and a small number!
